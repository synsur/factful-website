How every fact is checked.

Last updated 13 November 2026

The short version. Factful - Daily Facts is a free iPhone and iPad app that puts one short, surprising fact on your lock screen each day. Every fact names the page it was checked against, and every number in it is matched against that page before it ships. A fact that can't be matched is cut, never softened. If you find a mistake, Spot an error? on every fact comes straight to us.

Where the facts come from

Many begin as claims other people have already checked: items from Wikipedia's "Did you know" archive, which its editors choose for interest and review against their citations before they run, and from its list of common misconceptions, where every item is cited. Others come from NASA, NOAA, the US Geological Survey, the Nobel Prize, Smithsonian, university news offices and peer-reviewed journals.

To be exact: of the 431 facts in the app on 13 November 2026, 346 cite a Wikipedia article and 85 cite another source. Each one links to the exact page it was checked against, under Why it's true, so you can read it yourself.

True isn't enough

A fact has to be surprising, concrete, still true, and clear to someone who knows nothing about the subject. Claims that are contested, out of date or rest on "scientists believe" are turned down, unless the fact is the correction of a popular myth. If reputable sources disagree, the fact is dropped.

In our last batch, 2,800 candidates became 917 that their sources confirmed, and 290 that were surprising enough to ship.

Every number, matched to its source

Before a fact ships, a script opens its source page and confirms that every number in the fact appears there. When a site blocks scripts it reads the Internet Archive's copy, and it reads PDFs as text. Only what it flags is looked at again: a page that won't open, a number that isn't on the page, or a fact with no numbers, whose page is read in full.

Some numbers are right but can't match word for word, like 99.73%, which is 364 days out of 365. Each of those carries a note in our records saying why. A fact that still fails after one repair is dropped. There are always more facts.

Videos are checked again

Each fact we post on TikTok and Instagram is checked again before its video is made. Every spoken line, every word on screen and the caption has to trace back to the fact itself, or to a sentence that appears word for word on a source page fetched for that video, with every number in the claim inside that sentence. Finding the number somewhere else on the page isn't enough. A line that doesn't match holds the video; it is never reworded just to get past the check.

A fact gets a page on this site only when that check passed on every line. Every fact we've posted is there, with its sources.

When we get one wrong

Every fact in the app has Spot an error? Tell us under Why it's true, and every fact page here has the same link. It opens an email that already says which fact you mean. We check the report against the source, and a confirmed error is corrected in a pinned comment on the post and in the app at its next update.

You can also write to help@factful.app. A real person reads every message.
